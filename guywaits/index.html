About this service

You cannot be in two places at once. I can be in the one that matters.

Some things are only sold to whoever is physically standing there. A boutique allocation, a numbered release, a Saturday morning drop with forty pairs and three hundred people. If you cannot fly to it, you do not get it, and no amount of refreshing a website changes that.

That is the whole service. You tell me what is dropping and where. I get myself there, I queue, and I buy it if buying is possible.

I pay for my own travel. Flights, hotel, trains, the taxi at 4am. It is folded into the quote and it is my problem, not a line of receipts I send you afterwards. That is also why I am happy to go somewhere far: the further and stranger the release, the fewer people bothered to show up, and the better your odds get.

How a booking works

Five steps, and one of them is you paying before I book anything

  1. You send the details. The item, the store, the city, the date, the local drop time, and your ceiling on price. The form at the bottom of this page collects exactly what I need.
  2. I check the mechanics and quote you. Every release has its own rules: first come first served, a wristband handed out the night before, a raffle you must register for, an appointment only a existing client can make. I find out which it is before either of us commits, and I tell you honestly what the odds look like.
  3. You pay the fee in full. Up front, before I book a single flight. This is not negotiable and it is not a deposit.
  4. I go and I queue. You get a message when I arrive, my position in the line, and a photo if you want proof I am actually standing in it.
  5. If it can be bought, I buy it. Purchase cost is separate from my fee and settled the moment it goes through. Then I ship it to you, insured, tracked.

Terms, stated plainly

What you are paying for is my time in a line, not the item

Read this part twiceA purchase is never guaranteed. I am selling you hours of standing in a specific place, and the chance that comes with being there. I am not selling you the watch.

Lines run out of stock. Raffles are drawn and lost. Boutiques decline sales at their own discretion, and some will only sell to a client whose name is already in their book. None of that is within my control, and none of it refunds my fee, because the work was done either way.

Payment is up front and non-refundable once I travel

The fee is due in full before I book anything. Once flights are paid for, that money is gone whether the drop goes your way or not. If you cancel before I book, I refund you minus anything already spent. If the release itself is cancelled or moved by the seller, I will roll the booking to the new date at no extra fee, travel costs permitting.

Enough notice, or I cannot promise anything at all

This is the single biggest factor in whether you get the item. Preparation is most of the job: booking sensible travel, confirming the drop mechanics, registering for whatever needs registering, and getting into the line early enough to matter.

Things I will not do

Not squeamishness, just the line between a proxy and a problem:

  • I will not pose as you where the seller forbids buying on someone else's behalf. Some boutiques do, and getting caught burns the store for both of us permanently.
  • I will not use bot accounts, bought queue positions, or fake identities to enter a raffle.
  • I will not hold a place for a reseller stack, meaning ten bookings for the same drop under different names.

If a release requires the purchaser to be present in person, or to hold an account or history the store can verify, I will tell you at the quote stage rather than take your money and find out on the day.

Fees

Quoted per job, because a Tuesday morning two hours away and a Friday night camp-out on another continent are not the same work. The quote is a single figure covering my time, the queue, my travel and shipping, and it does not move after you accept it. The item's own price is separate and settled at purchase.
